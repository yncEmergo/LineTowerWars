# Playtest 7: a link that stopped delivering, and freezes on first sight

**2026-09-10.** Five players on build `b900267` against the rented relay: three matches and a 1v1.
Reported afterwards: Linus "crashed" twice in the five-player match while the other four played on
untouched; "some lags at some point", possibly cai upgrading a lot of towers at once; and a laggy
opening for everyone, with the question of whether a grace period before input and timers would
help.

Two causes, neither of them the one it looked like. Linus's matches ended because ENet's reliable
channel stopped delivering on his connection while the rest of the link kept working, and the echo
cannot cover that. The lags were each player's own machine compiling a shader the first time a
kind of tower appeared on their screen - one to two seconds a time, then a fast-forward through the
backlog it left. Both are fixed, and both fixes are proven by a run that reproduces the failure.

## How it was read

- **Every file**: nineteen from five machines, the user's own session log from the office PC (the
  1v1, which they thought they did not have - now in the playtest folder as `milo_session-*.log`),
  and the relay's journal pulled from the server with `journalctl`. The journal was the key - it
  is the only record of what the relay SENT. The user's Godot log of the 1v1 is gone: Godot keeps
  five, and this investigation's own test runs rotated it out before it was copied. **Copy a
  machine's logs off it before running anything on it.**
- **One clock.** Each session log starts with its own UTC stamp, and the machines disagree by up
  to ten seconds - Linus's was about ten behind the relay, cai's about five. Aligned on events
  every machine saw at once, a player leaving being the clean one.
- **Three throwaway scripts**, all in the session scratchpad rather than the repo: a merged
  timeline; a detector for windows of ten turns that took over 0.75 s, aligned by TURN across
  players; and a test of whether a player's first build or upgrade into each kind of tower lines
  up with a freeze on that player's own machine.
- **Two reproductions**: the probe harness for Linus's failure, and `Scripts/Dev/ShaderProbe.gd`
  for the freezes.

## 1. Linus: a reliable channel that stopped, on a link that had not

What the relay's journal and Linus's logs show, in both matches:

- **The relay sealed and sent the turn.** The other four ran it.
- **Linus held every turn after it but that one.** `held` climbed at the full seal rate - 401 turns,
  the give-up ceiling, in twenty seconds - and `lockstep.gave_up` recorded `playing 772, sealed
  1188`. A buffer filling at twenty turns a second is the unreliable echo arriving. The turn it
  had missed never came, because by then no echo still carried it, and the reliable channel, which
  did, had stopped delivering.
- **match-3 did it twice running**: stuck at 799 for seventeen seconds, released as a burst, stuck
  again at 835, gave up. The connection itself closed twenty-three seconds later.
- **Idle in a lobby, the connection closed again**, with almost nothing on the wire. That one is
  the network alone.

His link against everybody else's, from each session's last health line:

| | round trip | echo recovered | stalls | held |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cai, both matches | 19 ms | 0 | 10 | 1.15 s |
| Gabriel, both matches | 28 ms | 0 | 15 | 1.30 s |
| hannes, both matches | 36 ms | 0 | 3 | 0.85 s |
| Azeo, match-3 | 37 ms | 0 | 8 | 1.75 s |
| Linus, match-2, 35 s of play | 128 ± 99 ms | 17 | 24 | 5.96 s |
| Linus, match-4, 12 min | 47 ms | 64 | 212 | 15.29 s |
| the user, match-4, 12 min | 34 ms | 0 | 1 | 0.56 s |

**Four of five players lost no seal at all, all session.** The echo field reported real loss on a
real link for the first time, and it was one player's. **The design goal held throughout**: none of
it reached the other four, whose matches carried on as if he were not there.

**Why ENet's channel stalls instead of recovering is NOT proven**, and there are two candidates.
ENet doubles a packet's resend timeout on every failed attempt, and any acknowledgement of OTHER
traffic resets its disconnect clock - so on a link with bursts of loss one unlucky packet can wait
tens of seconds without the connection ever dropping. Or datagrams above the path's MTU are being
thrown away while small ones pass, which would starve exactly the channel that batches. The logs
cannot tell them apart; the diagnostics below can.

**What the netcode got wrong**: the echo carries the last two turns, so a peer holding every turn
but one had no road to it except the channel that had stopped.

### The fix: seal repair

A peer missing the turn it needs next asks the relay for it by number, and the relay answers from a
history as deep as the give-up window, both over the unreliable channel. A HOLE - a later turn has
arrived and this one has not - is proof of a loss and is asked about almost at once; a turn merely
overdue with nothing later in hand waits longer before asking. The relay answers match players
only, rate-limits each, and keeps every reply under one packet. See
`LockstepService._repair_missing_seals`.

Reproduced on loopback with the probe harness: `--seal-loss 100` throws away every reliable seal on
arrival and `--echo-loss 20` a fifth of the echoes, which is Linus's link on demand. Forty-five
seconds of play, the joining peer:

| | turns played | stalls | held | gave up | repairs asked, seals repaired |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| clean link | 826 | 1 | 0.83 s | no | 0, 0 |
| Linus's failure, repair off | **12** | 2 | **41.67 s** | **yes** | 0, 0 |
| Linus's failure, repair on | 833 | 24 | 3.21 s | no | 24, 29 |

The host played every turn with one stall in all three. The repair-off run is playtest 7's crash to
the letter: 413 turns recovered by the echo, one missing, give-up. A clean link asks for nothing.
And a hostile third client that forged 5,423 repair requests alongside 5,423 checksums got nothing
served and no desync.

The hole wait started at 60 ms and was cut to 20 once the reasoning was checked: a hole means two
datagrams in a row went missing, the reliable copy was in the first, and ENet will not resend it for
a full round trip at least - there is nothing "just behind" worth waiting for. It bought 0.2 s over
the run. What remains, roughly 130 ms a hole, is structural: a lost turn cannot be known lost until a
later echo arrives.

### Diagnostics for next time

- The health line carries `repair` as `[asked, repaired]`, and `link` as ENet's own round trip,
  resend share and throttle for the road to the relay.
- **The relay logs its view of every player's link every thirty seconds**, at `info`, so the
  journal says what the relay's resends to each peer looked like while it happened. That is the
  direction every seal travels, and it is what separates the two candidates above.

Also fixed: the stall warning's `seconds` counted frames, and the catch-up servo had the engine at
three times speed through the stall - Linus's "100 seconds" was one minute. It is wall clock now.

## 2. The lags: shaders compiled the first time a kind of tower is drawn

**Every freeze was LOCAL.** A stall with a backlog behind it - fourteen to thirty-five turns held the
moment it cleared - is a machine that stopped, not a network that was late: the seals kept coming
and nothing played them. Then the servo fast-forwarded through them at three times speed, which is
the visible half of it.

Windows of ten turns that took over 0.75 s, where normal is half a second:

| | first 40 s of match-2 | the rest of match-2 | match-3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cai | turn 370, 1.20 s | 1590, 4970 | 8100, 1.23 s |
| Gabriel | 370, 2.13 s; 390; 690; 780 | 4310, 4610 | 2950; 7040 |
| Azeo | 80, 1.60 s; 390, 1.42 s | 2310, 1.55 s | 6900, 1.67 s; 7040, 1.57 s |
| hannes | none | none | none |

Three things pin it down.

- **They follow the camera, not the simulation.** Almost every one comes right after the player
  built, or upgraded INTO, a kind of tower for the first time in that process: the first tower of
  the match for cai, Azeo and Linus; Azeo's first Archer, first Lesser Crusher and first Runic
  Monolith; cai's seven Lesser Crushers at once, which is "cai upgrading a lot of towers". The same
  upgrade in somebody else's lane cost the others nothing. Every machine simulates every lane, so
  a simulation cost would have hit everybody on the same turn.
- **Only some new kinds froze.** Firepits, Cutters, Carvers, Firelords and a dozen more arrived
  without a hitch. The ones that froze are the ones that brought a new SHADER.
- **A warm process never froze at the start.** match-3 ran in the same processes as match-2 and its
  opening was clean. hannes, the one player on an AMD card, never froze at all. The user's own
  machine froze on nothing in the 1v1 and wrote no new entry to Godot's shader cache all evening -
  a dev machine that had compiled everything long before.

**The Compatibility renderer compiles a shader the first time something using it is drawn, on the
game thread**, and Godot's documentation says outright that the renderer has no other way: its
ubershaders and pipeline precompilation belong to Vulkan and Direct3D. `ContentWarmer` loads every
scene before the match and was never meant to compile anything. Godot keeps what it compiles on
disk, in `user://shader_cache`, so it is paid once per machine per shader - and again by every new
player, and by everybody after every change to a shader.

### Reproduced here

`Scripts/Dev/ShaderProbe.gd` stands every tower and creep model up for real, turns each into a build
ghost, plays every effect and shows every unit in the portrait - what play does - and counts the
files Godot's shader cache gains, which is one per compiled variant. Office PC, RTX 4080, with the
real cache moved aside so both runs start empty:

| | warm-up skipped | warm-up on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| variants compiled while drawing | 14 | **0** |
| worst frame, models and their ghosts | **1,023 ms** (the Archer) | 1.4 ms |
| worst frame, effects | 19 ms | 2.3 ms |
| the portrait | 0 | 0 |

The Archer's model alone froze the frame for a second, the Cannon's for half of one - on the
fastest card in the building with the driver's own shader cache warm. The first-tower freeze
playtest 7 saw is this.

### The fix: `ShaderWarmup`

Before the match starts it draws everything the match can show, once, behind a black screen: every
scene `ContentWarmer` holds, every drawable already standing in the match, what only exists once
somebody acts - the ghost, the markers, the bars, the range overlay, the blueprint, the bounty
number - and every unit again in the portrait. It holds the world still by name while it runs, and
**under lockstep the peer does not play a turn or tell the relay it is ready until it has
finished**, so the time is spent inside the wait everybody is already in for the slowest loader.
On an empty cache it took 1.2 s here, including loading the content a single player match had not
loaded; on a cold testers' machine it is unmeasured, and the session log's `match.shaders_warmed`
reports it from the next playtest.

The portrait compiled nothing of its own after the match world had - its lights need no variant the
match's do not. It is warmed anyway, because that is a fact about today's lighting rather than a
rule.

### On the grace period

The opening lag was these freezes - the first tower, the first ghost and build grid, the first
creeps and shots - and not the timers. A grace period changes when the match's clocks start, which
is a rule, and it would not have prevented a single one of the freezes above: the towers that caused
them were built after any grace period would have ended. Worth testing the warm-up first.

## 3. Smaller things

- **Every exported client prints about two hundred "invalid UID" warnings at boot.** The source
  `.tres` files carry no uid at all, so the uids are made at export and do not match the pack's own
  cache; every load falls back to the path and works. Noise and a little boot time, not a fault.
- **The seal clock waits for the slowest loader**, 2.3 s in match-2. By design (B2), and now also
  where each peer's warm-up runs.
- **Linus's match-3 session log never reached the logs folder** - only its Godot log. Not chased.
- The probe runs print sixteen engine warnings at exit, "Leaked instance dependency". At exit only,
  after the warm-up had long finished. Not chased.

## What was changed

- `LockstepService`: seal repair on both ends, an echo fault injector, wall-clock stall seconds, the
  relay's link report, and the ready gate that waits for `ShaderWarmup`.
- `NetworkConfig`: the repair switch and its three timings, and `debug_echo_loss_percent`.
- `NetworkService.link_quality` and the health line's `repair` and `link` fields.
- `ShaderWarmup`, new, with small hooks in `Main`, `ContentWarmer`, `UnitModel`, `VisualEffect3D`,
  `BlueprintOverlay`, `UnitPortrait` and `UnitPanel` - each exposing how it builds what it draws, so
  the warm-up draws exactly that rather than a copy that could drift.
- The probe harness: `--seal-loss`, `--echo-loss` and `--no-repair`, a spoof that also forges repair
  requests, and repair counts in its result. `ShaderProbe`, new and kept.

**Two new rpcs change the protocol's signature**, so an old client and a new relay refuse each other
by name. The server and the client have to move together.

## What is still open

- **Which of the two candidates stalls ENet's reliable channel.** The relay's link report and the
  health line's `link` field say, from the next playtest on a link like Linus's.
- **How long a flaky player may vanish before losing the match**: ENet's own timeout, the relay's
  silence timeout and the give-up ceiling together decide it. With the sealed stream nobody waits on
  a missing player, so this is a question about the missing player's match rather than everyone
  else's - and it is the user's to decide.
- **Repair against a real link**, and the warm-up's cost on a machine that has compiled nothing.
- The stale uids in the exported content.

## Traps

**A LOADED MATERIAL IS NOT A COMPILED ONE.** `ContentWarmer` did everything it was built to and the
freezes were still there. Under the Compatibility renderer only a draw compiles a shader. Now in
`CLAUDE.md`, with the rule it leaves behind for anything that builds a material in code.

**A DEV MACHINE'S SHADER CACHE HIDES EXACTLY THE COST A PLAYER PAYS.** The office PC had compiled
everything months ago, froze on nothing, and wrote nothing new to its cache all evening. An empty
cache is the only honest baseline.

**THE ECHO COVERS A LOST PACKET, NOT A STOPPED CHANNEL.** Redundancy riding next to a stream only
repairs what it still carries. A stream that stops needs asking.

**A DIP ON ONE MACHINE IS DRAWING; A DIP ON EVERY MACHINE AT THE SAME TURN IS SIMULATING.** Every
peer simulates every lane, so aligning the slow windows by turn across players says which of the two
a hitch is before anything is profiled.

**FIVE MACHINES' CLOCKS DISAGREED BY UP TO TEN SECONDS.** Read in local time, the timeline has
players reacting to events before they happened. Align on something everybody saw.

**A FRAME COUNT IS NOT A DURATION WHILE SOMETHING CHANGES THE FRAME RATE.** The servo changes the
engine rate, and a stall measured in frames came out two-thirds too long.
